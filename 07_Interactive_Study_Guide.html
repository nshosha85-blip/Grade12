1Learning objectives & vocabulary

By the end of this lesson you can:

  • State the law of conservation of momentum for an isolated system (no external net force).
  • Explain why momentum is conserved using Newton's third law.
  • Solve collisions in which objects stick together: (m₁ + m₂) vf = m₁v₁i + m₂v₂i.
  • Solve collisions in which objects bounce, finding an unknown final velocity.
  • Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic or perfectly inelastic using kinetic energy.
  • Solve recoil and explosion problems where the total momentum is zero.
  • Apply conservation of momentum to ballistic pendulums and two-dimensional collisions.

Key vocabulary

System
The group of objects we choose to study together.
Isolated (closed) system
A system with no external net force acting on it.
Law of conservation of momentum
In an isolated system, total momentum before = total momentum after.
Collision
A short interaction in which objects exert large forces on each other.
Elastic collision
Momentum AND kinetic energy are both conserved; objects bounce.
Inelastic collision
Momentum is conserved but some kinetic energy is lost (to heat, sound, deformation).
Perfectly inelastic collision
The objects stick together and move with one common velocity; the most KE is lost.
Explosion
Objects at rest fly apart; total momentum stays zero.
Recoil
The backward motion of a gun or launcher when it fires something forward.
Initial velocity (vi)
The velocity before the interaction.
Final velocity (vf)
The velocity after the interaction.
Ballistic pendulum
A block that catches a bullet and swings up, used to measure the bullet's speed.

1. The law of conservation of momentum

If no external net force acts on a system, its total momentum stays constant: total momentum before = total momentum after.

For two objects: m₁v₁i + m₂v₂i = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f. Momentum is a vector, so choose a positive direction and keep the signs. Internal forces (between the objects) cannot change the total.

2. Why momentum is conserved

During a collision, object 1 pushes on object 2 and object 2 pushes back with an equal and opposite force (Newton's third law). The forces act for the SAME time Δt.

So F Δt is equal and opposite for the two objects: Δp₁ = −Δp₂. Whatever momentum one object gains, the other loses — the total does not change.

3. Sticking together (perfectly inelastic)

The objects move off together with one velocity: (m₁ + m₂) vf = m₁v₁i + m₂v₂i.

A 2.0 kg cart at 6.0 m/s hits a 4.0 kg cart at rest and they stick: vf = (2.0 × 6.0) ÷ 6.0 = 2.0 m/s. Head-on: 3.0 kg at +4.0 m/s and 2.0 kg at −6.0 m/s stick → total p = 0 → they stop.

4. Bouncing collisions

When the objects separate, there are two final velocities. If you know one, conservation of momentum gives the other.

0.50 kg at 4.0 m/s hits 1.5 kg at rest; the 0.50 kg ball bounces back at 2.0 m/s: 2.0 = 0.50(−2.0) + 1.5 v → v = 2.0 m/s.

5. Kinetic energy: elastic or inelastic?

Momentum is conserved in EVERY collision of an isolated system. Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.

Compare KE = ½ m v² before and after. Sticking-cart example: 36 J → 12 J (inelastic, 24 J lost). Bouncing-ball example: 4.0 J → 4.0 J (elastic).

6. Recoil and explosions

If everything starts at rest, the total momentum is ZERO before, so it is zero after: m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f = 0. The pieces move in opposite directions.

A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s: 0 = 0.010(400) + 4.0 v → v = −1.0 m/s (recoil). Lighter piece → faster; heavier piece → slower.

7. Ballistic pendulums and 2-D collisions

Ballistic pendulum: momentum is conserved in the collision, then energy is conserved in the swing: v = √(2 g h) for the block-and-bullet, then m(bullet) v(bullet) = (m + M) v.

In two dimensions, momentum is conserved separately in x and in y. A 1000 kg car going east at 20 m/s and a 1500 kg car going north at 10 m/s stick: px = 20 000, py = 15 000 → p = 25 000 kg·m/s, vf = 10 m/s at 36.9° north of east.

The 5-step routine
  1. Read — List masses and velocities; what is unknown?
  2. Sign — Choose + direction; sign every velocity.
  3. Before — Total momentum before = Σ m vi.
  4. After — Write total after with the unknown; set equal and solve.
  5. Check — Direction sensible? KE not increased (unless an explosion)?

Key ideas and formulas

  • Isolated system: p(before) = p(after)
  • m₁v₁i + m₂v₂i = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
  • Stick together: (m₁ + m₂) vf = m₁v₁i + m₂v₂i
  • From rest: m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f = 0
  • Elastic: KE conserved too
  • Inelastic: KE lost; momentum still conserved
  • Ballistic pendulum: v = √(2 g h) after
  • 2-D: conserve px and py separately

Common mistakes

⚠ Ignoring direction
Opposite motion → negative velocity.
⚠ Adding masses wrongly
Stuck objects: use (m₁ + m₂) vf.
⚠ "KE is always conserved"
Only in elastic collisions.
⚠ "Momentum is lost in a crash"
Total momentum is always conserved if isolated.
⚠ Explosion: total ≠ 0
From rest, total momentum stays zero.
⚠ Ballistic pendulum in one step
Momentum in the collision, energy in the swing.
